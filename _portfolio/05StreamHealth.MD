# About the Project

My semester-long stream health research project in Rāpaki, New Zealand, examined freshwater ecosystem condition 
using both Mātauranga Māori and Western scientific approaches. Working at three sites within the 
Whakaraupō catchment, I collected water-quality measurements and macroinvertebrate samples to compare 
streams with different levels of restoration and human impact. I analyzed community composition using 
the Quantitative Macroinvertebrate Community Index (QMCI) and NMDS ordination, alongside a 
Mahinga Kai assessment informed by Mātauranga Māori. The project explored how ecological indicators can 
complement cultural knowledge when evaluating freshwater health and restoration success. Through this work, 
I gained experience in field sampling, environmental data analysis, statistical visualization in R, 
and communicating interdisciplinary research findings.

This project was advised by Dr. Channell Thoms during my semester at the University of Canterbury.
